# Product note — ReportFix

**Who loses most when a question is broken?** The student who encounters the defect while preparing under time pressure loses the most: marks, confidence and scarce revision time. A wrong answer key is especially damaging because it can teach a correct student to distrust their own reasoning. ReportFix therefore prioritises answer-key disputes, repeat reports and defects that affect the validity of the question. It groups duplicate reports so faculty see the scale of an issue without treating repeated claims as proof.

**What should AI never auto-apply?** It should never invent or replace diagrams, create new distractors, decide syllabus inclusion, move a question to a different chapter without faculty confirmation, or resolve an unsound question where multiple/no options may be correct. It should not alter an answer key solely because a student says their answer differs. Even taxonomy-approved answer-key repairs need independent working and a traceable reason. Human-only taxonomy repairs remain gated.

**Week-one metric:** *Verified defect time-to-containment* — median time from the first student report to a verified correction or safe quarantine. This measures whether the product reduces the period in which a known defect continues harming students.

**Beyond the brief:** Add a “question health fingerprint” that combines report patterns, answer-selection anomalies, rendering telemetry and cross-question duplicate detection. It could surface broken questions before a student reports them, while keeping all proposed content changes behind the same evidence and faculty gates.
